# Analysis: lakshya1 against final and against panav3 (12 Nov 2026)

Written for: the owner (panav), to decide what to do about lakshya1's win over final. Numbers come from the 1000-hand
(stage 2) matches on seeds 1-18, the same seeds for both bots. Section numbers 1-8 below are generated by
`eval/lakshya_report.py`; rerun it when seeds 19 and 20 of the panav3 run finish (command at the end).

## Summary

1. **lakshya1 beats final over 1000 hands**: -0.637 chips/hand (95% CI -1.14 .. -0.14, excludes zero), final won 5 of 18
   matches. At 400 hands (70 matches, round 1) the two were level (+0.015, CI +-0.40). The edge grows with match length.
2. **The leak fix (panav3 = final + overbetdef) made it worse**: -1.292 chips/hand, 1 match won of 18, and -0.655 +- 0.50
   chips/hand against final on the same seeds (better on only 5 of 18 seeds).
3. **The fix did exactly what it was built to do, and that is why it lost**: against 1.2-2.5x pot flop bets final's fold rate
   went 59% -> 50% (turn 62% -> 53%, river 73% -> 66%) and "flop we fold" improved by +0.31 chips/hand. But the extra
   calls go to showdown and lose: river showdowns went +0.31 -> -0.37 c/h (-0.68), turn showdowns -0.24, flop showdowns
   -0.14. Calls on 1.2-2.5x flop bets averaged +2.9 chips before (a selected, strong set) and -6.0 after: the marginal hands
   we added are losers. So the blueprint's heavy folding against overbets was **not** the leak I guessed; folding those
   hands is roughly right. The log numbers that made it look like a leak (calls average positive) were selection bias.
4. **What lakshya1 actually does to final** (section 6): it takes the initiative. It bets 70% of its flop decisions, 53% of
   turn, 37% of river, with a mean size of 1.2-1.4x pot and 59-75% of those bets at or above one pot. Final bets 33-38% of
   decisions at 0.54-0.64x pot and raises only 5% of the time it faces a bet. Final therefore folds 50.4% of all hands against
   lakshya1's 33.4%, and every fold-ending costs a lot (flop -1.35, turn -1.37, river -1.07 chips/hand-equivalent, section 3).
   Showdowns for final were even in the 17-match breakdown (won 1,319 hands for +70,625 chips, lost 1,315 for -70,046):
   the whole loss is who folds.
5. **Where final may gain instead (a hypothesis, not tested)**: lakshya1 folds a lot to final's bets once they come:
   43% on the flop, 54% on the turn, 73% on the river (section 6), and the pot odds final offers with its 0.5-0.65x bets are
   about 33-39%. Folds at those rates are well above what makes bluffs break even. Final's bet frequency is the lever (it
   bets 33-38% of the time), not its defence. The existing `overfolder` trait (flop/turn, p0 0.45 / p1 0.65) fired in only 1 of
   36 legs against lakshya1 (measured rate 0.50), so the current layer does not exploit this.
6. **Position**: panav3's loss against lakshya1 is much larger on the button (-1.37 c/h) than final's (-0.40), section 7.
   I have not investigated why.

## What I did not establish
- Why lakshya1 beats final more as matches get longer (adaptation by its opponent model vs variance). A split of each match
  into first and last 200 hands would test it; not run.
- lakshya1's own fold and bluff frequencies by hand strength (the logs only show hands that reach showdown).
- Anything about other opponents: the zoo has not been run for panav3, and nothing here argues for shipping it. `dist/bot.py`
  is unchanged.
- panav3 numbers are for 18 of 20 seeds (the last two matches were still running).

## Suggested next step
Drop `overbetdef` (keep it off; it is off in every default build). Try a bet-more move against opponents that fold to our
turn and river bets above roughly 55%: more bluffs and thin value where the blueprint already bets sometimes, gated by an
SPRT trait on the opponent's fold rate to our river bets. Test it on the same 20 seeds against lakshya1, then the zoo.
